Project 1 · CS180

Images of the Russian Empire

Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii collection by aligning three monochrome glass-plate exposures, from exhaustive pixel matching to a coarse-to-fine pyramid and gradient-based alignment.

Part 1

From Glass Plate to Color

Each scan stores three filtered exposures vertically in B, G, R order. I split the plate into equal thirds, keep blue fixed as the reference, translate green and red, and stack the aligned channels into an RGB image.

  1. Step 01Split the plate into blue, green, and red grayscale channels.
  2. Step 02Search candidate x/y translations against the blue reference.
  3. Step 03Shift the green and red channels by their best offsets.
  4. Step 04Stack R, G, and B, then crop and enhance the result.

Part 2

Single-Scale Alignment

For the small JPEG scans, I exhaustively search all translations in a $[-15,15]$ pixel window. Only the overlapping interior is scored, and the outer 10% is ignored so that plate borders do not dominate the match.

L2 / Mean Squared Error

Lower values indicate a closer match. My implementation uses the mean squared difference, which is an SSD-style variant of L2.

$\operatorname{MSE}(I,R)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_p(I_p-R_p)^2$

Normalized Cross-Correlation

Higher values indicate a closer match. Mean subtraction makes NCC less sensitive to a global brightness offset.

$\operatorname{NCC}(I,R)=\frac{(I-\bar I)\cdot(R-\bar R)}{\lVert I-\bar I\rVert\lVert R-\bar R\rVert}$

Part 3

Coarse-to-Fine Image Pyramid

A direct large-window search is too expensive for the full-resolution TIFFs. I recursively downsample both channels by a factor of two with anti-aliasing, search ±30 pixels at the coarsest level, double that estimate at each finer level, and refine it within ±5 pixels. The same fixed parameters are used for every image.

  1. CoarsenDownsample until the largest dimension is below 300 pixels.
  2. SearchEstimate the displacement on the smallest pair of images.
  3. PropagateMultiply the coarse displacement by two for the next level.
  4. RefineSearch a small neighborhood and repeat at full resolution.

Why it is faster

The broad displacement is found where the images are small. Full-resolution work is limited to a local 11×11 search rather than hundreds of candidate shifts in each direction.

Measured runtime

On the collected baseline run, pixel NCC typically took 15–19 seconds per TIFF and pixel L2/MSE took about 8–9 seconds, remaining below the one-minute target.

Part 5 · Better Features

Aligning Edges Instead of Intensities

The same physical surface can have very different values through red, green, and blue filters. This breaks the raw-pixel assumption most clearly on Emir. I implemented 2D convolution and Sobel, Prewitt, and Roberts filters from scratch, then run NCC on gradient magnitude. Structure remains comparable even when color response does not.

A decisive failure case

Green aligns under all methods, but the red exposure is too dissimilar from blue for raw-pixel matching. The pyramid then amplifies an incorrect coarse estimate.

Part 7 · Bells & Whistles

Automatic Finishing

After alignment, I apply three automatic finishing steps: valid-overlap and dark-border cropping, global contrast stretching, and Gray World white balance.

Automatic Cropping

First, the G/R offsets define the common valid rectangle, removing wrapped pixels created by np.roll. Sobel row and column profiles then search only the outer 20% for a dark-to-image transition. A brightness check prevents a strong interior edge from being selected solely because of its gradient.

Automatic Contrast

One global linear mapping is shared by all RGB channels, preserving their relationship while mapping the darkest value to zero and the brightest to one.

$I'=\frac{I-I_{\min}}{I_{\max}-I_{\min}}$

Gray World White Balance

The average RGB color is assumed to be neutral gray. Each channel receives a multiplicative gain that brings its mean to the shared mean. Statistics are accumulated in float64 to avoid visible error on ten-million-pixel images.

Current Limitation

The crop detector is designed for dark borders. Bright or strongly colored rims can remain, as visible in several results. This is preferable to aggressively cutting genuine image content.

Part 8

Failure Analysis

Emir: the red and blue intensities differ so strongly that both raw-pixel NCC and L2 choose implausible coarse offsets. Gradient magnitude restores a shared structural representation and produces a clean alignment.

Church: the L2 pyramid repeatedly selects the edge of its local search region, ending at (555, 555). NCC succeeds because normalization is less sensitive to the channel-level brightness difference.

Moving subjects: color fringes around people in Harvesters and Three Generations are not purely registration errors. The three exposures were captured at different moments, so subject motion cannot be corrected by one global translation.

Plate borders: the conservative cropper removes detected black edges and invalid overlap but intentionally leaves uncertain bright or colored bands. A more general border-consistency model would be needed to remove every rim safely.

Failed baseline offsets are preserved in the table instead of being manually corrected. They motivate the final Sobel-NCC method and make the comparison reproducible.

Conclusion

A Reusable Alignment Pipeline

The final system combines exhaustive translation search, a recursive image pyramid, NCC on Sobel gradient magnitude, valid-overlap cropping, contrast stretching, and Gray World white balance. The basic metrics work on most plates; the failures reveal why feature choice matters as much as search strategy.
